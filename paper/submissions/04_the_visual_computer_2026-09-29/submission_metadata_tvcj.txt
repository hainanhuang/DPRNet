Dynamic Prototype Routing for Lightweight Image Super-Resolution

Abstract
--------
Lightweight single image super-resolution (SR) must balance reconstruction
quality with model cost on resource-constrained platforms. Content-aware
routing improves this balance by grouping semantically related tokens, yet
the widely used center-based router--the Token Aggregation Block (TAB) of
CATANet--relies on cross-batch exponential-moving-average centers, hard
assignment labels, and a coupled prototype update/confirmation step, tying
routing to a drifting historical state and discarding assignment confidence.
We revisit token routing itself and propose Dynamic Prototype Routing (DPR), a
confidence-aware, drop-in replacement for the TAB routing that keeps the
CATANet backbone and tensor interface unchanged. DPR builds per-image
prototypes directly from the current tokens, decouples prototype generation
from confirmation through a learnable query bank, matches tokens with a
bounded learnable temperature, and carries the resulting assignment
confidence into token ordering, global gating, and a soft fallback, with a
usage-balancing regularizer that discourages prototype-slot collapse. Trained
on DIV2K and evaluated on five standard benchmarks at x2/x3/x4, DPRNet matches
CATANet-level accuracy with small gains on most benchmark cells while staying
in the lightweight regime; at x4, for example, it obtains 26.89 dB on Urban100
with 52.14 G Multi-Adds, fewer than the lightweight Transformer baselines
SwinIR-light and SRFormer-light under the same input convention. Three-seed
ablations isolate each design choice: the dynamic prototypes outperform a
history-center EMA variant under an identical protocol, and the balancing
loss makes prototype usage more balanced and more stable across seeds. DPR
thus offers a reusable, interpretable alternative to center-based token
routing rather than a larger backbone.
