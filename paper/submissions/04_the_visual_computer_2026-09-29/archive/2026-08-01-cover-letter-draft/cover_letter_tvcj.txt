Cover Letter — The Visual Computer

August 1, 2026

Dear Editor-in-Chief and Editors of The Visual Computer,

We are pleased to submit our manuscript entitled "Dynamic Prototype Routing for
Lightweight Image Super-Resolution" for consideration as a research article in
The Visual Computer.

Single image super-resolution is a core visual-computing building block: it recovers
fine detail for display, rendering, and downstream visual analysis, and it is
increasingly deployed on devices where memory, latency, and energy are tightly
constrained. This deployment setting makes lightweight SR — a favorable trade-off between
reconstruction quality and model cost — the practically relevant regime, and it is the
regime this paper targets.

Content-aware token routing is a promising way to reach that trade-off, and the recent
CATANet is a strong representative: its Token Aggregation Block (TAB) groups semantically
related tokens before attention. Our contribution is at the level of the routing
mechanism rather than at the level of network capacity. We show that the TAB router has
four concrete, analyzable weaknesses — cross-batch exponential-moving-average (EMA)
centers that lag per-image content, hard argmax assignment labels that discard
confidence, an intra-cluster ordering with no purity constraint, and an entangled
prototype update/confirmation step — and we replace it with Dynamic Prototype Routing
(DPR), a drop-in module that keeps the CATANet backbone and its external tensor interface
unchanged. The main contributions are:

  1. Dynamic, per-image prototypes formed directly from the current tokens by a soft,
     assignment-weighted aggregation, removing the cross-batch EMA center buffer.
  2. Decoupled prototype confirmation via a learnable query bank, separating prototype
     generation from confirmation for more stable and interpretable routing slots.
  3. Confidence-aware routing: the retained per-token assignment confidence modulates
     token ordering, a global gate, and a soft prototype fallback.
  4. A learnable, bounded routing temperature together with a usage-balancing
     regularizer that prevents prototype-slot collapse.

We are deliberately transparent about the scope of our claims, since we believe this is
where the contribution is best judged and evaluated. On five standard benchmarks at
x2/x3/x4, DPRNet matches CATANet-level accuracy with small but consistent gains on most
benchmark cells (e.g., 26.89 dB on Urban100 at x4). DPR adds a modest cost over CATANet
(660K vs 535K parameters; 52.14G vs 46.8G Multi-Adds under a common 256x256-input
protocol), so we do not claim an efficiency advantage over CATANet; rather, DPRNet stays
firmly in the lightweight regime and uses markedly fewer Multi-Adds than the lightweight
Transformer baselines SwinIR-light (60.3G) and SRFormer-light (56.5G). The value of DPR
is that it trades this small, well-characterized cost for an interpretable,
confidence-aware routing mechanism. To support this, we report multi-seed (3-seed, mean
+/- std) ablations that (i) directly show dynamic prototypes outperform a re-implemented
EMA history-center variant under a matched protocol, and (ii) show the balancing loss
makes prototype usage both more balanced and more stable across seeds, together with
routing diagnostics (usage entropy, learned temperature, per-token confidence, and
per-block cluster-assignment maps) that make the learned routing visually interpretable.

Because DPR is a plug-and-play, interface-preserving module, it is directly reusable in
other window-attention visual-computing backbones. We believe this combination — a
reusable content-routing component, an honest efficiency/accuracy characterization, and
mechanism-level, visually interpretable analysis on a practically important task — fits
the scope and readership of The Visual Computer well.

This manuscript is original, has not been published previously, and is not under
consideration for publication elsewhere. All authors have read and approved the
manuscript and agree to its submission. The authors declare no competing interests. The
datasets used (DIV2K, Set5, Set14, BSD100, Urban100, Manga109) are all publicly
available; no new data were generated in this study.

We suggest the following areas of expertise for reviewers: lightweight/efficient image
super-resolution, vision Transformers for image restoration, and content-aware token
routing / clustering.
